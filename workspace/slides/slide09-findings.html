Key Findings

1

Model-specific diversity varies dramatically

There is no single "AI diversity" story. Claude is more diverse than humans, Gemini moderately less, and GPT-5.2 dramatically less. Aggregating across models produces misleading results.

2

GPT-5.2 exhibits mode collapse

Near-identical proposals with virtually zero internal variation across all 23 generations. Effect sizes hit the theoretical maximum (|delta| = 1.0) on pairwise and centroid measures.

3

Claude produces diverse subclusters

High overall diversity but with local clustering. Explores different proposal "modes" (indicated by subclusters in UMAP), yet within each mode, proposals share similarities. Zero outliers despite highest spread.

4

Human proposals uniquely occupy shared and novel territory

Moderate diversity with genuine outliers (13%). 56.5% of humans' nearest neighbors are AI proposals, meaning humans overlap with AI space. But humans also produce truly unique ideas not found in any AI output.
