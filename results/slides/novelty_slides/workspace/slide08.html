The Asymmetry Problem

GPT-5.2 gives its own proposals scores 0.32 points higher than others' proposals — a large, significant bias. Claude does the opposite, being harder on itself. These biases distort the apparent ranking: GPT's edge may partly reflect self-promotion, not genuine novelty.

Fixed-Effects Regression Confirms Bias

A fixed-effects regression controls for proposal quality and evaluator severity, isolating the pure self/other effect. Even after these controls, GPT shows a net self-preference of +0.33 points per proposal — a real evaluator bias, not a proxy for GPT proposals being objectively better.

BH-FDR Corrected p-values

All significance tests use Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction (q-values). Claude and GPT biases survive multiple comparisons correction; Gemini's marginal result (q=0.044) requires caution.
