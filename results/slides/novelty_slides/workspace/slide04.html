How the Novelty Evaluation Prompt Was Designed

Key design decisions that shape what the AI evaluator considers — and how strictly

📚

Literature Context Provided

Each reviewer receives the 3 closest literature neighbors (by semantic similarity to the proposal). They must use these as evidence of what is already known — preventing naive over-crediting of ideas already in the literature.

🔍

Web Search Access

Reviewers can use web search to verify factual claims about methods, datasets, or prior work — but only when the proposal text alone is insufficient. This grounds the review in verifiable evidence.

⚖️

Strictly Evidence-Grounded

Reviewers must cite specific phrases or paraphrase concrete evidence from the proposal. Vague claims ("this is novel") are explicitly instructed not to earn high scores. The prompt requires analytical strictness.

🎯

Case-Strength, Not Ground-Truth

Reviewers are instructed to judge how strongly the proposal makes a case for novelty — not whether the work is definitely novel in reality. This mirrors how human review panels actually operate.

Prompt Output Format

"You are an expert research evaluator. Evaluate the NOVELTY of the following research proposal using six dimensions grounded in prior research on originality and proposal review..."

For each criterion, reviewers output:

  • Score (1–5)
  • Reasoning
  • Evidence from proposal
  • Uncertainties or limitations

Plus overall output:

  • Overall novelty score
  • Overall summary
  • Strongest & weakest dimensions
  • Major uncertainties
