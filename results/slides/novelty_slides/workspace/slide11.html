Conclusions

Putting It Together: A Coherent Story on AI Novelty Review

Four findings that complement each other — the full picture requires holding all four at once

01

Raw Scores Favor GPT-5.2 Proposals

GPT-5.2 proposals receive the highest overall novelty scores (M=3.99), above human proposals (M=3.76) and Claude proposals (M=3.40). On raw numbers, AI proposals — especially GPT's — appear more novel to AI reviewers.

02

But Evaluators Differ Dramatically

A 1-point spread exists between the most lenient (Gemini, M=4.18) and strictest (Claude, M=3.18) evaluators — larger than the entire human-GPT score gap. Raw comparisons are confounded by who is doing the rating.

03

Self-Preference Amplifies the Pattern

GPT inflates its own proposals (+0.32 pts, large effect); Claude deflates its own (−0.40 pts, large effect). These opposing biases artificially widen the apparent GPT–Claude quality gap across all six novelty criteria.

04

GPT Advantage Is Real, But Smaller

After removing self-evaluations, GPT-5.2 proposals still score significantly higher than human proposals (δ=−0.87, p<0.001). The bias amplified but did not fabricate this effect. Claude proposals become indistinguishable from humans.

Big Picture: AI evaluators reveal genuine differences in how AI-authored vs. human-authored proposals articulate novelty — but those evaluators are themselves biased. Trustworthy AI review requires cross-evaluator designs that neutralize self-preference, plus awareness that evaluator leniency can swamp any author-group signal.
